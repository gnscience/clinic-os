The problem

Catch the gap before the claim goes out

Specialty operators lose money when authorization, unit, credential, and documentation issues are found only in denial and appeal cycles. Clinic OS moves that work upstream.

01

Authorization and unit gaps

Gates check auth status and remaining units before the session is booked or billed, so the claim is not the first place the problem appears.

02

Credential and visit readiness

Operational checks surface credential and visit-readiness issues while there is still time to fix them, not after submission.

03

Documentation that backs the claim

Claim-ready state tracks what the session needs on the operational record, so documentation gaps are visible before the claim leaves the clinic.

04

Cited recommendations, human dispose

Every rule-backed recommendation carries its citation. Agents propose; licensed clinicians and coders decide.

The platform

Clinic OS: three pillars over one operating system

Built on a knowledge kernel that cites every rule, an operational record of your own, and specialty packs designed for the way your clinic delivers care.

01

Ontology-network kernel

A cited knowledge kernel: terms, rules, and evidence paths that agents and humans can inspect. Every rule-backed recommendation carries its citation. Nothing is a black box.

02

Operational system of record

The record we own: auth, session, credential, visit, and claim-ready state. The EMR and clearinghouse stay as peripherals. Clinic OS is the system of record for operations.

03

Specialty and capability packs

Home health, pediatrics, ABA, and capability packs for revenue integrity, coding, and ops intelligence, all layered on the same OS. Sequencing is strategy. The architecture is shared.

Agents propose. Humans dispose.

Observe, recommend, then act. Human-in-the-loop is the default control plane, not a footnote.

No clinical decisioning, diagnosis determination, or treatment planning under any framing. Coding candidates and operational gates only; licensed clinicians and coders decide.

What Clinic OS is not

  • Not an EMR replacement
  • Not rip-and-replace
  • Not a black-box coder
  • Not autonomous billing without a human
  • Not a clinical decision tool

Specialty packs

First wedges on a shared OS

Home health and pediatrics are first-wedge candidates. ABA is the hero demo path. The OS stays constant; packs specialize the surface.

First-wedge candidate Specialty pack

Home health

Visit integrity, credential and EVV adjacency, and operational gates suited to agency workflows, without replacing the agency EHR.

First-wedge candidate Specialty pack

Pediatrics

Ops integrity and shared substrate for pediatric clinical operations, not clinical EHR features. Identity and eligibility substrate that other packs can ride on.

Hero demo Specialty pack

ABA

Unit ledger, authorization gates, and claim-ready session state for applied behavior analysis operations, the path we demo first.

On the roadmap: HCBS waivers, hospice, and therapy (OT/PT/SLP) packs on the same OS.

Capability packs

Revenue integrity · Cited diagnosis coding · Ops intelligence

Capability packs sit on Clinic OS. They are not the product; Clinic OS is.

Capability pack

Revenue integrity

Gate denials before the session. Align authorization, unit ledger, and claim-ready state so leakage is caught at the operational edge instead of chased in appeals.

Capability pack

Cited diagnosis coding

A capability that keeps recommendations inside a defensible candidate set, each with an evidence path. Reviewers see why, not just what. Coders decide.

Capability pack

Ops intelligence

Cited recommendations over the operational record. Surface what to act on across scheduling, credentials, and claims, with a human disposing on every material step.
